By day it is a cozy first-person shepherd sim: you name your sheep, lead them to green pasture and still waters, anoint them with oil and bind their wounds. By night one of them goes missing, and you leave the ninety-nine to find it. ## Key features
1. **Care is power.** Feeding, watering, anointing and binding wounds raise each sheep's Trust. At Trust 60 that sheep adds its own bond card to your night deck.
2. **Leave the ninety-nine.** Every night is about one specific, named sheep, the one least bound to you.
3. **Your voice.** The Call herds the flock by day and is a card by night. Sheep that know your voice follow it.
4. **The lamp is your clock.** Each step below burns oil. Run dry and you walk the Valley of the Shadow: vision shrinks and enemies strike first.
5. **A narrator at the table.** A BG3-style storyteller voices both halves, sets each place, names the stakes, and jokes.
6. **One satchel, two worlds.** Oil, linen, hyssop and smooth stones gathered by day are your consumables below. An oil flask spent on a sheep's head is light you won't have tonight.
7. **Weather with rules.** Fog halves your call range, rain lets sheep drink anywhere, and a red sky at evening means fair tomorrow.
8. **A storybook world.** Flat-shaded paper-cut 3D, ink-hatched dungeons and hand-built paper cards.
9. **Five lands.** The flock moves on through Green Pastures, the Dry Country, the Oasis, the High Snows and the Valley of the Shadows. Each land has its own weather and needs by day, and its own beasts below (scorpions, a crocodile, a wild boar, shades).
10. **A flock that is yours.** Hats, scarves, flower crowns and angel wings for the sheep, and a cuddle button. None of it does anything; that is the point.

## FAQ answers
**Is it a religious game?** It's inspired by the shepherd passages of the Bible (Psalm 23, John 10, Luke 15) and keeps them in the mechanics rather than in sermons. You don't need to share the faith to enjoy it, and players who do share it will recognise it everywhere.

**Can sheep die?** No. Sheep can be lost and frightened, never killed. The night is a rescue, not a punishment.

**Do I have to be good at deckbuilders?** No. A gentle night setting gives more oil and longer enemy telegraphs. Deckbuilder players can take a "rest day" to skip chores.

**How long is a day / night?** About 8–10 minutes of daylight and a 10–20 minute descent.

**Who is the narrator?** A wry, warm, second-person storyteller in the BG3 tradition who runs both halves like a game master.
