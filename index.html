The game

Two games that need each other.

By day you tend a flock of named sheep in sunlit hills. By night one is missing, and you leave the ninety-nine to find it. Every bond you make in daylight becomes a card in the dark.

By day

  • Name, feed and water your flock
  • Bind wounds, anoint with oil
  • The Call: sheep follow a voice they know
  • Gather oil, linen, hyssop, stones
  • Dress them up, or just hold one a while

By night

  • First-person grid descent
  • Lamp oil is your clock
  • Turn-based card combat
  • Beasts that change with the land
  • Find the one. Carry it home.

Good Shepherd is two games that need each other. By day it is a cozy first-person shepherd sim: you name your sheep, lead them to green pasture and still waters, anoint them with oil and bind their wounds. By night one of them goes missing, and you leave the ninety-nine to find it. The descent is a first-person grid crawler with turn-based deckbuilder combat, where every sheep that trusts you adds its own card and your lamp oil is your clock. A wry, warm narrator tells the whole thing like a tabletop game master. Care is power. There is no grinding, only love.

FAQ

Can sheep die?

No. Sheep can be lost and frightened, never killed. The night is a rescue, not a punishment.

Is it a religious game?

It draws on the shepherd passages of the Bible (Psalm 23, John 10, Luke 15) and keeps them in the mechanics rather than in sermons. You don't need to share the faith to enjoy it.

Do I need to be good at deckbuilders?

No. A gentle night setting gives more oil and longer enemy telegraphs.

What makes it Good Shepherd

Care is power. No grinding, only love.

1Bonds become cards

Every sheep that trusts you adds its own card to your night deck. Neglect the flock and you descend with a thin hand.

2Leave the ninety-nine

Each night is about one specific, named sheep: the one least bound to you.

3Your voice

The Call herds the flock by day and is a card by night. Sheep that know your voice follow it.

4The lamp is your clock

Every step below burns oil. Run dry and the Valley of the Shadow closes in.

5One satchel, two worlds

Oil spent anointing a sheep by day is light you won't have tonight.

6Weather with rules

Fog halves your call range. Rain lets sheep drink anywhere. A red sky at evening means fair tomorrow.

7Five lands

The flock moves on: green pastures, dry country, an oasis, high snows, and the Valley of the Shadows. Each has its own needs by day and its own beasts below.

8A flock that is yours

Straw hats, scarves, flower crowns, a pair of angel wings. They do nothing at all, and neither does a cuddle. That is the point.

A narrator at your table

Told like a tabletop campaign.

In the tradition of Baldur's Gate 3, a wry, warm narrator speaks to you in the second person. She sets each new place, names the stakes, and has opinions about every sheep. She runs the cozy days and the dungeon nights like a game master at your table.
